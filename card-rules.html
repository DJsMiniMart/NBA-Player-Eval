Quick start

  1. Open your 5 free starter packs. That's 15 cards. Your best 10 fill your lineup automatically: 5 starters, 5 on the bench.
  2. Set your lineup before the first game tips off each night. Make sure your five fit together (see Chemistry) and your bench covers every position (see Bench balance).
  3. Wake up to results. Once every game from the night is final, your five plays three games against real box scores: Fantasy Five, vs Pulse, and vs a real NBA team.
  4. Earn points from those games, from your picks on The Morning Press, and from a daily check-in bonus.
  5. Spend points on packs, sell cards whose value has gone up, and climb the weekly ladder for free packs.

Your roster

  • 10 spots: a starting five and a five-man bench. Any other cards you own sit in your collection and don't play.
  • One of each player in the lineup. You can own duplicates, but the same player can't be in your lineup twice.
  • Your first lineup is set for you: the best guard and best center from your starter packs start, then the three next-best players; the next five go to the bench. Change it any time with the Start, Bench and Out buttons on each card.
  • Lineup lock: a night is played with the lineup you had before that night's first tip-off. Changes after the first game starts count from the next night. The page tells you which night your changes count for.
  • No hard position rules. You can start any five. But lineups that don't fit together are penalized heavily (see Chemistry).

Cards

Overall rating (OVR)

Every card's OVR comes from where the player's Pulse ranks this season among qualified players (at least half as many games as the league leader, and 15+ minutes a game). It runs from about 60 to 99, steepest at the top, and updates as the season goes: a player who heats up gets a higher OVR on every copy of his card. Early in the season, players with few minutes are pulled toward the league middle so one hot night doesn't make a 95.

3 · Nightly games: +10 per win

Up to 40 points a night: 10 each for winning Fantasy Five, vs Pulse and vs a real team, plus 10 for an upset. Details below.

Nightly games

Every night with NBA games, your lineup plays three games, all decided by that night's real box scores. Results show up once every game from that night is final (usually the next morning). Nights with no games, or before your team existed, don't count.

Your score: Fantasy Five production

Each of your five players earns his Pulse production for the night (game totals, not per minute):

PTS + 2×AST + 2×REB + 4×STL + BLK − 4×TOV
A 30-point, 10-rebound, 5-assist, 2-steal, 1-block, 3-turnover night = 30 + 10 + 20 + 8 + 1 − 12 = 57.

Add up your five, then apply your chemistry bonus or penalty. That's your Fantasy Five score, and it's the number used in all three games.

Who plays: subs

A starter who doesn't play that night (injured, resting, or no game) is replaced by the first bench player at his position who did play: a guard for a guard, a wing for a wing, a big for a big. If there's nobody at that position, the first bench player who played comes in. A sub's production is adjusted by your bench balance. If you run out of subs, the empty spot scores 0.

Game 1 · Fantasy Five

Reach 120: +10. Most nights land somewhere between 80 and 160, so it takes a good night from a well-built five.

Game 2 · vs Pulse

Pulse builds a five from players who played that night (none of yours), with about the same total OVR as your starters. Beat its total production: +10. Pulse's five gets no chemistry adjustment, so a well-built lineup has the edge.

Game 3 · vs a real team

Your five against the actual starting five of the NBA team whose starters' average OVR was closest to yours that night. Beat their total production: +10. If their starters were rated higher than yours and you still won, that's an upset: another +10. Their five gets no chemistry adjustment either.

Ties count as losses in the head-to-head games: you have to outscore the other five.

Chemistry

You can start any five you want, but they have to work together on a real court. Chemistry rates your five from 0 to 100, based on their season averages, and it's measured against real NBA starting fives (which score about 70 to 86).

PartWeightWhat scores well
Positions30%About 2 guards (PG/SG), 2 bigs (PF/C) and a wing (SF). Each guard or big too many or too few costs a quarter of this part.
Touches25%One ball to share. The five's shots + free-throw trips + turnovers per game should total about 65 to 85. Above 85, it falls off fast and reaches zero at 110: five stars who all need the ball fight over it.
Playmaking15%Assists: none below 12 a game combined, full marks at 20.
Spacing15%Threes made: none below 5 a game combined, full marks at 10.
Rim & boards15%Half blocks (1.5 to 4 a game combined), half rebounds (20 to 32).

A center and four star forwards: great players, but no guard, too few assists and too many shots. Chemistry 38 (−20%) plus no guard (−20%) = −40%. They'd need 200 production just to reach 120.

Balanced stars (a point guard, a shooting guard, a wing, a power forward and a center): chemistry 75 = +5%. A balanced lineup of role players can hit 80+ and get the full +10%, because they don't fight over the ball.

A full night, start to finish

Your starters: a PG, an SG, an SF, a PF and a C. Chemistry 72. Bench balance 100. Your starting SG sits with a sore ankle.

  1. Sub: the first guard on your bench who played comes in for the SG. He has 22 production; bench balance +5% makes it 23.
  2. Production: 23 + your other four (38, 29, 31, 24) = 145.
  3. Chemistry is rechecked with the sub in the five: still 72, so +5% → 152.
  4. Fantasy Five: 152 beats 120 → +10.
  5. vs Pulse: Pulse's five had 138 → you win, +10.
  6. vs a real team: the closest starting five by OVR was rated a little higher than yours and had 149 → you win +10 and the upset +10.

That's a perfect 3–0 night and 40 points. The 152 also goes toward your weekly ladder total.

Your record and saving your team

  • Scoreboard: your win-loss record in each of the three games, your last 10 nights, current and best streaks, this week's record, best night, average score, perfect nights and upsets.
  • Every night is saved, so you can scroll back through past results. If you don't open the page for a few days, those nights are scored when you come back, using the lineup you had for each night.
  • Saving: your team stays saved when you close the browser. Sign in (Menu → Settings, or the button under the scoreboard) to keep it in your account and play from any device. Without signing in, it lives only on that device; use Backup code to keep a copy.

Strategy

  • Fit beats stars. A balanced five with chemistry 80 gets +10%; five stars at chemistry 38 with no center lose 40%. That's a 50% swing.
  • Count touches. Two or three players who need the ball, plus a passer, a shooter and a rim protector who don't, is the sweet spot.
  • Check the injury report before tip-off. A starter who sits is replaced from your bench, so a healthy starter beats a slightly better one who's questionable.
  • Keep a backup center. It's the bench spot that saves the most nights.
  • Watch values. Sell a hot bench card at its peak and put the points toward a Galaxy pack.
  • Hit the picks. All three lines right in one game (138 in the example above) is worth almost a full Gold pack.

FAQ

My starter's team didn't play tonight. What happens?

Same as an injury: the first bench player at his position who played subs in.

Can I change my lineup during the night's games?

Yes, but the change counts from the next night. The night already underway uses the lineup you had before its first tip-off.

A player got traded. Is my card still good?

Yes. Cards follow the player, and his rating and value keep updating on his new team.

Why did a card's OVR change?

OVR follows the player's Pulse rank, which moves every day. Every copy of his card updates together.

Why doesn't my result show up yet?

A night is scored only when every game from that night is final, so a late West Coast game can push results to the next morning.

Can I play against a friend?

Not yet. Head-to-head between friends is on the way.
